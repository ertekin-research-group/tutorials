06 — Band Structure Week 5

The SCF then non-SCF workflow, a band structure along a high-symmetry path, and your first encounter with the DFT band gap problem.

Goal. Produce a silicon band structure, read the indirect gap off it, and understand why that gap is roughly half the experimental value — and why that matters for everything you will do with defects.

1. Why this takes two calculations

A band structure is the set of electron energies En(k) along a path through reciprocal space connecting high-symmetry points. You cannot get that from one ordinary calculation, because of a conflict:

  • A self-consistent calculation needs a uniform k-mesh, because it integrates over the Brillouin zone to build the charge density.
  • A band structure needs points along a line, which is a terrible sampling for that integral.

The resolution is to split the work in two:

Stagek-pointsWhat it doesKey tag
SCFUniform mesh Converge the charge density and save it to CHGCAR. LCHARG = .TRUE.
non-SCFLine mode Read that fixed charge density and just evaluate eigenvalues along the path. ICHARG = 11
What ICHARG = 11 means. "Read the charge density from CHGCAR and do not update it." The second calculation is not self-consistent at all — it solves the Kohn-Sham equations once, in the fixed potential produced by the first. That is both legitimate and much cheaper: the density is a Brillouin-zone integral, so it was correct to get it from a uniform mesh, and eigenvalues at any given k depend on the potential rather than on the other k-points.
The two runs must be numerically identical apart from the k-points. Same ENCUT, same POTCAR, same PREC, same structure. Change any of those between the SCF and the non-SCF step and the eigenvalues you get do not correspond to the density you computed. The INCARs in scf/ and nscf/ both carry the same ENCUT placeholder for this reason — change both, to the same value.

2. Step 1 — prepare the inputs

This module starts from the relaxed structure you produced in module 05:

cd tutorials/06_si_bands
conda activate defects
python prep_bands.py

prep_bands.py does four things, and it is worth reading because it is a compact tour of pymatgen:

  1. Reads ../05_si_relax/CONTCAR — your relaxed structure.
  2. Converts the 8-atom conventional cell to the 2-atom primitive cell.
  3. Writes that primitive POSCAR into both scf/ and nscf/.
  4. Generates a line-mode KPOINTS for nscf/ along the standard FCC high-symmetry path.
Why switch to the primitive cell? Band structures are conventionally plotted in the primitive Brillouin zone. Using the 8-atom conventional cell would fold the bands — the same physics, but with four times as many bands crossing each other, and a plot that does not match any textbook figure. It is also four times cheaper.

Then copy POTCARs into both directories:

cp ~/vasp_pseudo/Si/POTCAR scf/POTCAR
cp ~/vasp_pseudo/Si/POTCAR nscf/POTCAR

And set your converged ENCUT in both scf/INCAR and nscf/INCAR.

3. Step 2 — the SCF run

cd scf
./run_local.sh

Note the two tags that differ from module 05:

  • LCHARG = .TRUE. — save the charge density. This is the whole output that matters.
  • NSW = 0, IBRION = -1 — static. The structure is already relaxed; do not move it again.

Confirm the CHGCAR exists and is not empty:

ls -lh CHGCAR

4. Step 3 — the non-SCF run

cd ../nscf
cp ../scf/CHGCAR CHGCAR
./run_local.sh
You must copy the CHGCAR yourself. With ICHARG = 11 and no CHGCAR present, VASP does not necessarily stop with a clear error — it may fall back to something else and give you a plausible-looking, wrong band structure. Check the file is there before you run.

This run is fast — there is no SCF loop to converge, just one diagonalization per k-point along the path.

5. Step 4 — plot

cd ..
python plot_bands.py

This produces Si_bands.png and prints the gap, the VBM and CBM locations, and — the actual point of the module — a comparison against experiment.

What a correct silicon band structure looks like

  • The valence band maximum sits at Γ.
  • The conduction band minimum sits near X, not at Γ. Hence indirect.
  • The valence band width is roughly 12 eV.
  • Four valence bands (eight valence electrons in the 2-atom cell).
Check the indirect character explicitly rather than eyeballing it. The script prints both the VBM and CBM k-point indices — if they are the same, something is wrong with your path or your structure.

6. The band gap problem

Your computed gap will be around 0.6 eV. The experimental value is 1.17 eV. You are low by roughly a factor of two.

This is not a mistake, and it is not fixable by converging harder. Semi-local functionals like PBE systematically underestimate band gaps, typically by 30–50%. The cause is a real deficiency of the approximation, not a numerical one. Raising ENCUT or the k-point density does nothing for it — you already converged those in module 04.

Notice what PBE gets right

The gap magnitude is badly wrong, but the topology is correct: the gap is properly indirect, the VBM is at Γ, the CBM is near X, and the valence band dispersion is quite accurate. PBE gets the shape roughly right and the gap size badly wrong. That pattern is general, and it is why band-gap corrections are often applied as a rigid shift rather than a recalculation.

Why this dominates defect physics

Defect levels are reported relative to the band edges — "0.9 eV below the conduction band minimum". If your gap is only half as wide as it should be, there is only half as much room in which to place that level, and every charge transition level you compute inherits the error. This is the single biggest reason the modern defect literature uses hybrid functionals such as HSE06.

And it is much worse for our actual materials than for silicon. GaN's experimental gap is 3.4 eV and AlN's is 6.2 eV; PBE will underestimate both severely, AlN dramatically so. You will measure exactly how badly in Week 6.

What we will do about it

For this semester, work in PBE deliberately. It is cheap enough to let you learn the entire workflow — supercells, charge states, finite-size corrections, chemical potentials — and every one of those methodological steps is identical regardless of functional. In Week 15 you will run HSE06 on the bulk hosts, confirm the gaps come out close to experiment, measure the cost, and use that to plan the spring.

Set this expectation for yourself now. When your Week 11 transition levels disagree with the published hybrid-functional numbers, this is why. That disagreement is anticipated, and the right response is to quantify and explain it — not to assume you have a bug. Every student hits this and quietly assumes they broke something.

7. Deliverable

  1. Si_bands.png, committed, with readable axes and a caption that stands alone.
  2. The computed gap, the experimental value, and the ratio.
  3. A short written statement identifying the VBM and CBM locations and confirming the gap is indirect.
  4. A paragraph on the band gap problem in your report: what it is, why it is not a convergence issue, and why it matters for defect levels specifically. You will reuse this paragraph — write it properly now.

Before moving on

  • My band structure has the VBM at Γ and the CBM near X.
  • I used the same ENCUT and POTCAR in both the SCF and non-SCF runs.
  • I understand what ICHARG = 11 does and why two runs are needed.
  • I can state my gap, the experimental gap, and the ratio.
  • I can explain why a denser k-mesh will not fix the gap.
  • I understand why this specifically corrupts computed defect levels.
